# V4.1 Diversity Revision

Date: 2026-09-30

Status: CANDIDATE CORPUS — NOT TRAINED

## What changed

V4.0 exposed a severe template-concentration problem:

- 10,000 rows;
- 947,118 normalized tokens;
- 735 unique normalized tokens;
- 0 hapax tokens;
- top 100 tokens = 65.82% of token mass;
- top 100 bigrams = 31.00%;
- top 100 trigrams = 23.01%;
- 70 unique six-token response prefixes;
- maximum prefix reuse = 800 rows.

V4.1 keeps the same ten behavioral families and exactly 1,000 rows per family, but changes the synthesis strategy rather than merely adding superficial paraphrase wrappers.

The candidate now uses:

- 100 grounded scenario cases across 20 domains;
- 10 request modes;
- 5 cognitive levels;
- 6 lexical registers;
- 12 response frames;
- 6 family-specific behavioral lenses;
- deterministic scenario-linked response composition;
- zero model/API dependency;
- reproducible GitHub Actions generation;
- explicit scenario coverage in the manifest.

The 100 scenarios are distributed across software, research, operations, data, security, projects, writing, education, history, science, product, finance, legal, healthcare, manufacturing, research software, knowledge, communication, planning, and everyday decision contexts.

## Research basis

The revision follows several independent findings from current instruction-data research.

Yang et al. (ACL 2025) report that a useful diversity measure should account for both inter-sample differences and information density, and propose NovelSum as a sample-level novelty measure.

Chen et al. (ACL 2025) report that information-gain-based selection in semantic space can improve instruction-data selection, and caution that simple embedding-distance heuristics can miss instruction intent.

Riaz et al. (ACL 2025) report that template-based synthetic generation can produce degraded downstream results relative to more diverse synthesis, even when the template varies exemplars.

Kambhatla et al. (EMNLP 2025) report that synthetic instructions can be substantially less diverse than human-written instructions and that merely adding fine-grained persona detail does not guarantee large diversity gains.

Deshpande et al. (EMNLP 2025) report that diversity selection can be confounded by output length, motivating the length-aware measurements retained here.

Schaffelder and Gatt (ACL 2026) report that diversity of synthetic-data sources can affect distribution collapse and output diversity.

These studies motivate the architecture and measurements. They do not establish that V4.1 will improve Vera after training.

## Hostile review

> **HOSTILE REVIEWER:** V4.1 is still templated. You have simply made the template larger.

**Response — partially accepted.** The revision remains compositional by design. That is unavoidable for deterministic zero-cost generation. The important change is that the repeated semantic unit is now the scenario grid rather than a handful of response fragments. V4.1 has 100 grounded scenarios, 5 cognitive levels, and 10 request modes, and the structural measurements moved materially in the desired direction. This does not prove semantic diversity, so the next gate remains semantic evaluation.

> **HOSTILE REVIEWER:** The 1,006-token vocabulary is still tiny for 10,000 responses.

**Response — accepted.** V4.1 is substantially better than V4.0 but still narrow. The corpus should not be treated as diversity-complete. The next revision should expand scenario vocabulary and add a semantic-distance gate rather than declaring victory.

> **HOSTILE REVIEWER:** The lower sampled 4-gram Jaccard could simply mean the responses are shorter or less coherent.

**Response — accepted as a possible confound.** V4.1 responses average 3.415 sentences versus 5.06 in V4.0. The gzip ratio and Distinct-N gains provide additional structural evidence, but neither establishes quality. Length-controlled comparison and behavioral-quality checks are required before training.

> **HOSTILE REVIEWER:** Why not train V4.1 immediately and let the model decide whether the data is good?

**Response — rejected.** Training would destroy experimental clarity. The current corpus can be improved and compared at zero weight-change cost. A candidate should clear structural and semantic review before compute is spent.

## Training-mixture correction

The training-builder verification exposed a separate arithmetic drift in the V4 lineage.

The V4 rehearsal builder's checked-in target set sums to 40,500 rows, while the V4 training builder expected a 42,500-row rehearsal pool. With 10,000 custom rows, 2,000 rehearsal validation rows, and 500 custom validation rows, the actual train pool was therefore 48,000 rather than the declared 50,000.

V4.1 does not silently accept that mismatch. It adds a separate pinned rehearsal wrapper that increases four already-selected public source splits by 500 rows each, restoring an exact 42,500-row rehearsal pool.

The resulting arithmetic is now:

- 10,000 V4.1 custom rows;
- 42,500 rehearsal rows;
- 52,500 source rows;
- 500 custom validation rows;
- 2,000 rehearsal validation rows;
- 50,000 training rows;
- 2,500 validation rows.

The first 42.5k rehearsal build passed the split contract; persistence failed only because another workflow pushed the branch concurrently. Training-manifest persistence has since been made retry/rebase tolerant.

## Behavioral review finding — V2 rejected

The first deterministic 100-row review sample exposed a real quality defect that the embedding metrics did not catch.

Two classes of errors were found:

- prompt grammar errors such as "Please what should the response say?" and incorrect articles such as "a analytical";
- unsafe lexical substitution in responses that changed grammatical category or meaning, producing malformed phrases such as "Do not proposition..." and "Do not assertion...".

This is a concrete example of why diversity metrics cannot substitute for response-quality inspection. The V2 candidate is therefore rejected as a training artifact.

The fix is V3:

- remove lexical substitution from behavioral content entirely;
- retain surface variation through complete response-prefix forms rather than word-level replacement;
- repair prompt article selection deterministically;
- remove the malformed request-to-imperative transformation;
- add regression tests for the observed defects;
- keep the scenario-grounded application sentence;
- version the resulting corpus and 50k training mixture as V3.

The review sample itself is now generated deterministically at `successor/corpus/v4_1/behavioral_review_set.jsonl` with 10 examples per behavioral family.

This review was not an independent human study; it is model inspection of a deterministic sample. It is evidence that the V2 generator had defects, not proof that V3 is defect-free.

## Behavioral review finding — V3 still rejected

The V3 grammar correction fixed the malformed English found in V2, but the deterministic review exposed a deeper semantic problem: the 20-domain scenario grid was still largely generic across all ten behavioral families.

For example, an identity-stability response could be paired with a budget worksheet or editing note whose actual issue was unrelated to identity. The response was internally coherent as an identity lesson, but the scenario did not instantiate the behavior being trained.

That is insufficient for a behavioral SFT corpus.

V4 changes the scenario generator itself:

- the same 20 domains are retained;
- each family now has five behavior-specific scenario conditions;
- every family therefore has 100 distinct grounded cases = 20 domains × 5 behavior conditions;
- the base domain case is retained so domain diversity remains real;
- the behavioral condition is appended to make the scenario instantiate the family being trained;
- the response application sentence now has a concrete behavioral situation to act on rather than merely decorate.

This follows the same general direction as recent synthetic-data work emphasizing situated, real-world-grounded instructions rather than trivial surface variation. Zhu et al. (ACL 2025) specifically describe grounding instructions in situated users and meaningful real-world situations; the paper reports that this approach produced diverse instructions that improved downstream benchmarks. https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.517/.

V3 is therefore a rejected intermediate artifact. V4 is the current candidate.

## Behavioral review finding — V4 rejected

The V4 review sample exposed one remaining family-specific contamination defect in `negative_transfer_resistance`: its inherited core contained a concrete arithmetic answer ("408"). That answer was itself correct in its original source task, but it is not an appropriate reusable behavioral target when the V4 scenario is about an unrelated current task.

This is a form of semantic leakage: the response teaches the intended negative-transfer principle while simultaneously carrying a stale task answer into unrelated examples.

V5 removes that concrete answer and replaces all five negative-transfer cores with task-agnostic principles:

- answer the current task directly;
- retain only prior context that changes the answer;
- use the current task's style and constraints;
- exclude unrelated earlier context;
- prevent prior emotional or identity-heavy framing from distorting an unrelated task.

V5 is the current candidate. V2, V3, and V4 are rejected intermediate generator revisions.

## Behavioral-quality gate

The structural and semantic-diversity gates are not sufficient by themselves. A synthetic response can become more diverse while becoming less useful.

The current generator therefore changed its response construction so the response contains an explicit application sentence tying the behavioral lesson to the concrete scenario and requested cognitive operation. The earlier V4.1 construction merely appended a scenario statement; that was too weak because the scenario could be present without being meaningfully acted upon.

This change is motivated by recent instruction-tuning research emphasizing instruction-response alignment. MAIN (EMNLP 2025) reports that synthetic instruction quality depends on mutual alignment between the instruction and response rather than the quality of either component in isolation: https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.644/. FedDQC (Findings of ACL 2025) likewise describes instruction-response alignment as a low-cost quality signal for filtering training data: https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.791/.

The new zero-cost gate measures, on the same deterministic sample for V4.0 and V4.1:

- prompt-response cosine similarity;
- mismatched-prompt/response cosine similarity using a deterministic permutation;
- alignment margin = own-prompt similarity minus mismatched-prompt similarity;
- fraction of examples whose own prompt beats the mismatched prompt;
- the existing semantic clustering metrics.

The shuffled-prompt control is important: raw prompt-response similarity alone can be inflated by generic language. A useful response should be more aligned to its own instruction than to a randomly paired instruction.

The gate is diagnostic first. It does not yet define an absolute universal quality threshold, because the embedding model is itself a proxy and the target behavior is project-specific.

## Next gate

Do not change model weights yet.

The next useful work unit is a semantic-diversity gate that can run without paid APIs. Options include:

1. a local embedding model if a zero-cost runner can obtain one legally and reproducibly;
2. a deterministic TF-IDF/cosine proxy as a lower-bound structural-semantic signal;
3. nearest-neighbor novelty scoring inspired by NovelSum;
4. stratified sampling checks across domain, cognitive level, request mode, and family;
5. length-controlled comparison against V4.0;
6. a small manually inspectable review set for semantic drift.

Only after V4.1 survives those checks should a V4.1 training mixture be constructed.

No model weights, main branch, deployment state, credentials, or paid compute were changed.
