# V4.1 Diversity Revision

Date: 2026-09-30

Status: CANDIDATE CORPUS — NOT TRAINED

## What changed

V4.0 exposed a severe template-concentration problem:

- 10,000 rows;
- 947,118 normalized tokens;
- 735 unique normalized tokens;
- 0 hapax tokens;
- top 100 tokens = 65.82% of token mass;
- top 100 bigrams = 31.00%;
- top 100 trigrams = 23.01%;
- 70 unique six-token response prefixes;
- maximum prefix reuse = 800 rows.

V4.1 keeps the same ten behavioral families and exactly 1,000 rows per family, but changes the synthesis strategy rather than merely adding superficial paraphrase wrappers.

The candidate now uses:

- 100 grounded scenario cases across 20 domains;
- 10 request modes;
- 5 cognitive levels;
- 6 lexical registers;
- 12 response frames;
- 6 family-specific behavioral lenses;
- deterministic scenario-linked response composition;
- zero model/API dependency;
- reproducible GitHub Actions generation;
- explicit scenario coverage in the manifest.

The 100 scenarios are distributed across software, research, operations, data, security, projects, writing, education, history, science, product, finance, legal, healthcare, manufacturing, research software, knowledge, communication, planning, and everyday decision contexts.

## V4.0 → V4.1 structural comparison

V4.1 currently measures:

- tokens: 917,185 vs 947,118;
- unique tokens: 1,006 vs 735;
- TTR: 0.001097 vs 0.000776;
- Distinct-2: 0.004591 vs 0.002897;
- Distinct-3: 0.008805 vs 0.004602;
- Distinct-4: 0.013441 vs 0.006348;
- top-100 token mass: 63.11% vs 65.82%;
- top-100 bigram mass: 29.69% vs 31.00%;
- top-100 trigram mass: 22.61% vs 23.01%;
- gzip response ratio: 10.26% vs 1.82%;
- sampled 4-gram Jaccard: 0.011495 vs 0.075330;
- unique six-token response prefixes: 334 vs 70;
- maximum six-token prefix reuse: 710 vs 800.

These are structural signals, not proof of better model behavior. The semantic metric is still missing. The sampled 4-gram Jaccard is explicitly lexical/structural, not semantic similarity.

## Research basis

The revision follows several independent findings from current instruction-data research.

Yang et al. (ACL 2025) report that a useful diversity measure should account for both inter-sample differences and information density, and propose NovelSum as a sample-level novelty measure.

Chen et al. (ACL 2025) report that information-gain-based selection in semantic space can improve instruction-data selection, and caution that simple embedding-distance heuristics can miss instruction intent.

Riaz et al. (ACL 2025) report that template-based synthetic generation can produce degraded downstream results relative to more diverse synthesis, even when the template varies exemplars.

Kambhatla et al. (EMNLP 2025) report that synthetic instructions can be substantially less diverse than human-written instructions and that merely adding fine-grained persona detail does not guarantee large diversity gains.

Deshpande et al. (EMNLP 2025) report that diversity selection can be confounded by output length, motivating the length-aware measurements retained here.

Schaffelder and Gatt (ACL 2026) report that diversity of synthetic-data sources can affect distribution collapse and output diversity.

These studies motivate the architecture and measurements. They do not establish that V4.1 will improve Vera after training.

## Hostile review

> **HOSTILE REVIEWER:** V4.1 is still templated. You have simply made the template larger.

**Response — partially accepted.** The revision remains compositional by design. That is unavoidable for deterministic zero-cost generation. The important change is that the repeated semantic unit is now the scenario grid rather than a handful of response fragments. V4.1 has 100 grounded scenarios, 5 cognitive levels, and 10 request modes, and the structural measurements moved materially in the desired direction. This does not prove semantic diversity, so the next gate remains semantic evaluation.

> **HOSTILE REVIEWER:** The 1,006-token vocabulary is still tiny for 10,000 responses.

**Response — accepted.** V4.1 is substantially better than V4.0 but still narrow. The corpus should not be treated as diversity-complete. The next revision should expand scenario vocabulary and add a semantic-distance gate rather than declaring victory.

> **HOSTILE REVIEWER:** The lower sampled 4-gram Jaccard could simply mean the responses are shorter or less coherent.

**Response — accepted as a possible confound.** V4.1 responses average 3.415 sentences versus 5.06 in V4.0. The gzip ratio and Distinct-N gains provide additional structural evidence, but neither establishes quality. Length-controlled comparison and behavioral-quality checks are required before training.

> **HOSTILE REVIEWER:** Why not train V4.1 immediately and let the model decide whether the data is good?

**Response — rejected.** Training would destroy experimental clarity. The current corpus can be improved and compared at zero weight-change cost. A candidate should clear structural and semantic review before compute is spent.

## Semantic gate result

A zero-cost embedding comparison was run with `sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2` using the same deterministic 600-response sample for V4.0 and V4.1.

V4.1 reduced the sampled semantic clustering signals:

- mean nearest-neighbor cosine: 0.859622 → 0.750731;
- median nearest-neighbor cosine: 0.864734 → 0.752595;
- p90 nearest-neighbor cosine: 0.921089 → 0.846849;
- mean pairwise cosine: 0.396080 → 0.302931;
- fraction of sampled pairs at cosine >= 0.90: 0.000440 → 0.000017.

This is materially stronger evidence than the earlier lexical-only pass, but it is still model-based sample evidence rather than ground truth. The comparison used the same embedding model and sampling scheme for both corpora.

The semantic receipt is committed at `successor/corpus/v4_1/custom/semantic_diversity_comparison.json`.

## Next gate

Do not change model weights yet.

The next useful work unit is a semantic-diversity gate that can run without paid APIs. Options include:

1. a local embedding model if a zero-cost runner can obtain one legally and reproducibly;
2. a deterministic TF-IDF/cosine proxy as a lower-bound structural-semantic signal;
3. nearest-neighbor novelty scoring inspired by NovelSum;
4. stratified sampling checks across domain, cognitive level, request mode, and family;
5. length-controlled comparison against V4.0;
6. a small manually inspectable review set for semantic drift.

Only after V4.1 survives those checks should a V4.1 training mixture be constructed.

No model weights, main branch, deployment state, credentials, or paid compute were changed.
